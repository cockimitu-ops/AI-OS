## What It Does
Generates original candidate premises with an inherent change and conflict (per [[Storytelling_Fundamentals]]), grounded in a specific fear category — the unknown, dread, body horror, or existential — rather than sourced from existing posts or stories. Rewritten this sprint: the previous version assumed a source pool of real material, from the earlier Reddit adaptation pillar; this version assumes nothing exists yet and has to be invented.

## Inputs
A fear category or subgenre direction (optional — can also generate broadly), and any recurring elements to consider reusing once Universe Support exists (not yet built).

## Outputs
A list of original candidate premises, each with a one-line hook concept, its fear category, and whether it reads as evergreen or trend-responsive.

## Success Criteria
- Every candidate premise names a change and a conflict, per [[Storytelling_Fundamentals]].
- Every candidate can articulate its "this could happen to me" angle — the personal-relevance lever that keeps curiosity high even on a large information gap, per [[Curiosity_Psychology]].
- No candidate is generated by lightly reskinning a specific, identifiable existing work — that's what [[Originality_Check]] verifies downstream, but ideation shouldn't be producing near-misses in the first place.

## Validation
Does each candidate have a nameable change, conflict, and personal-relevance angle? Reject any that don't before they reach [[Story_Validation]].

## Analytics Reference
Which fear categories and premise types actually produce validated, then successful, stories is an [[Experiment_Tracking]] question over time — there's no data yet to know which categories perform best for this audience.
