## Story_Drafting — Outline and the Multi-Part Reconciliation

This run is also where [[Horror_Story_System]]'s open reconciliation question (single-episode research model vs. multi-part existing structure) got resolved in practice, not just in theory. Applied here: **the five-phase model runs once across the whole story, and again, in miniature, within each part** — each part gets a fast local hook (for a viewer landing without context) plus its own escalation and a local cliffhanger or, for the final part, the resolution.

- **Part 1** (~270 words / ~110s): Hook → Setup → Escalation → local reveal ending on a cliffhanger (something's inside, not just outside).
- **Part 2** (~280 words / ~112s): Quick re-hook → escalation with a false-security beat → stronger cliffhanger (frozen frame + crash).
- **Part 3** (~270 words / ~108s): Quick re-hook → climax/reveal (the epiphany, reframing every earlier detail at once) → final sting (a smaller, new loop, reopened).

## The Script

### Part 1

My doorbell camera has been recording the same man standing outside my door for six hours a night. Every night this week. He's never rung the bell. He's never shown up on my neighbor's camera, ten feet to the left of mine.

I work nights. I get home around 4 AM, and out of habit, I always scroll back through the motion clips before I sleep. Tuesday, there he was. Just standing there, facing the door. No struggle to get in. No looking around. Just standing.

I checked the timestamp. He'd been there since 10 PM. Six hours, in one twenty-second motion clip that only triggers when something moves closer than three feet — which meant he wasn't moving at all.

I told myself it was someone waiting for a rideshare. Wrong house, maybe. I almost let it go.

Then I checked Wednesday's clip. Same time. Same spot. And Thursday.

I asked my neighbor if her camera had picked up anything, since it points almost the same direction as mine, just a few feet over. She sent me four nights of empty porch. Nobody there. Not blurry, not out of frame. Nobody.

Friday, I decided to stay up and watch the live feed instead of checking it later. Nothing showed up. Not at 10, not at midnight, not at 2. I fell asleep on the couch around 3.

I woke up to a notification. New clip, 3:14 AM — while I was still on the couch, ten feet from that door.

I opened it already knowing what I'd see.

He was facing the camera for the first time. And in the reflection off the lens — barely there, easy to miss if I hadn't been looking for it — there was a second shape.

Standing just inside my own front door.

### Part 2

Quick recap: for a week, my doorbell camera caught a man standing at my door for hours — never on my neighbor's camera, never ringing the bell. Then I found him facing the lens for the first time, with something reflected behind him. Inside my house.

I didn't sleep that night. I went through every camera I own — the doorbell, the smart plug light in the hallway, even the baby monitor app still installed from house-sitting my sister's place, pointed at my hallway out of laziness. Nothing on any of them. No second shape. No proof.

I told myself the reflection was a coat on the hook. Warped glass. My brain finding a face in nothing, same as clouds and toast.

I almost believed it, until I remembered that hallway coat hook has been empty since I moved everything to the closet three months ago.

I started sleeping with the hallway light on. Two nights, nothing. Third night, the hallway camera picked up motion at 3:14 AM again — same time as before — and caught four seconds of a shape at the end of the hall before the light apparently flickered and the clip cut to static.

I've started waking up in places I didn't fall asleep. Tuesday I fell asleep on the couch and woke up in my bed, door locked from the inside, like I always lock it. I don't remember doing either.

Tonight I set up a livestream instead of relying on clips, so I'd know the second anything moved. I'm watching it from my friend's apartment across town, door locked behind me, phone in hand.

At 3:14, I got the notification.

I opened the stream. One frame loaded before it froze — the shape, standing directly over my bed. Over where I would have been sleeping.

Then the app crashed. It didn't reconnect until morning.

### Part 3

Quick recap: my doorbell camera caught someone at my door every night for a week, then a second shape reflected just inside my house. I've been losing time. Last night I livestreamed my apartment from across town and watched the feed freeze on a shape standing over my bed, right before the app crashed.

This morning, I found out the stream had kept recording locally, even after it looked frozen on my end. It just hadn't been able to upload live.

I watched the recovered file at normal speed first. It's exactly what I saw — the frame freezes on the shape over the bed, then cuts. But local files don't cut. They just stop being interesting to look at unless you know to keep watching.

So I did. Frame by frame.

The shape doesn't move. It just stays there, leaning over the bed, for eleven full minutes. Around the nine-minute mark, there's audio. Barely audible. I slowed it to a quarter speed.

It's my voice.

Not the shape's. Mine. Saying something I have no memory of ever saying, in a room I wasn't conscious for, to someone standing over a body that was, as far as I can tell from every other angle, just me. Asleep. Alone.

I don't think there's ever been a man at my door. I think there's a version of me that gets up sometime after I fall asleep, that the hallway camera and the doorbell camera have been catching for a week, and that I have no memory of being.

I deleted every clip. I put the cameras in a drawer.

Last night, at 3:14 AM, my phone lit up anyway. New motion detected. Hallway camera.

I wasn't home.

## Video Review (Gemini)
Claude has no native video-watching capability, so once Part 1 was rendered, a review prompt was built for Gemini instead — same rigor as everything else in this record, not generic feedback. Covers hook timing, pacing, TTS naturalness, visual fit of the horror-gameplay background, caption legibility, and cliffhanger pull. Prompt used:

```
You're reviewing a short-form horror video (Part 1 of a 3-part TikTok/YouTube Shorts/Instagram Reels series) for production quality and retention effectiveness. This is TTS-narrated, first-person horror storytime content over gameplay footage background.

Watch the full video and assess it against these specific criteria. Be honest and critical, not just positive — vague praise isn't useful here.

HOOK (first 3 seconds):
- Does the disturbance/hook land within the first 3 seconds, or does it take longer to get going?
- Any wasted time before it — intro, branding, slow build — that should be cut?

PACING:
- Does tension escalate through the video, or are there flat stretches that feel repetitive?
- Does the background gameplay footage switch often enough to stay visually engaging (roughly every 3-5 seconds) without feeling distracting from the narration?

TTS/AUDIO:
- Does the voice sound natural at its current speed, or rushed/robotic anywhere?
- Any words that sound mispronounced or oddly emphasized?
- Does the audio rhythm track the tension — slower during calm moments, tighter during tense ones — or does it sound flat throughout?

VISUALS:
- Is the on-screen caption text legible (size, contrast, timing)?
- Does the background gameplay footage fit the horror tone, or feel tonally mismatched?

ENDING/CLIFFHANGER:
- Does the final moment genuinely create a pull toward Part 2, or does it feel like it just stops?
- Does anything resolve too much, or too little?

OVERALL:
- As a first-time viewer with zero context, would you keep watching past 3 seconds? Past 15?
- What's the single biggest change that would improve retention?

Be specific. If something doesn't work, say so plainly and explain why.
```

Results back — see full findings and fixes below.

### Findings
- **Hook:** Lands immediately, zero wasted time. Minor: bright Backrooms-yellow visuals at 0:00 undercut the unsettling premise.
- **Pacing:** Script escalation is structurally sound. Visual switch rate (2–4s) matches the target frequency without feeling hyperactive. **Real issue: visual incoherence** — footage swaps between unrelated games/environments (Backrooms, retro horror house, snowy outdoors, pixelated kitchen) that don't match the story's actual setting (a front porch/apartment door). Reads as abstract filler, not mood-setting.
- **TTS/Audio:** Understandable at 1.2x, but flat — no cadence variation. The reveal line ("...a second shape standing just inside my own front door") delivers at the exact same pace as the setup. **This is the per-segment speed variation already specified in [[CapCut_Production_Formatting]] not actually being applied in the render** — not a new finding, an execution gap.
- **Visuals/Captions:** Captions themselves are good — legible, high contrast. A handwritten-note overlay in the game footage collides with caption text for ~2 seconds around 01:12.
- **Ending/Cliffhanger:** The line itself is strong — clear pull toward Part 2. But the "FOLLOW FOR PART 2" text pops in before the narrator finishes speaking, cutting off the line's weight.
- **Overall:** Would hold a first-time viewer past 3 seconds and past 15 seconds. Completion rate assessed as moderate — the footage disconnect costs visual immersion even though the script and hook are strong.

### Fixes Needed Before Publishing
1. Replace all background footage with dark, grounded, first-person indoor/porch/hallway horror gameplay only — no Backrooms, no bright/mismatched environments. Fears to Fathom-style footage confirmed as the right fit; given this story's own premise (security camera footage), an Iron Lung-style camera-feed aesthetic is a specifically good match worth trying.
2. Fix the caption/overlay collision at ~01:12.
3. Add a ~0.4–0.5s pause before the reveal line — apply the per-segment TTS variation that was already specified but not executed.
4. Delay the "FOLLOW FOR PART 2" text by ~0.5s after the narrator finishes the final line; a quiet audio sting or brief silence first would help it land.
