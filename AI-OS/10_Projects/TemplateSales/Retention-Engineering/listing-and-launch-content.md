**Do I need a specific editor or TTS tool?**
No. Module 5 includes a settings block you fill in for whatever you use.

**Isn't this just "make good videos"?**
The opposite. It's a set of constraints: max 15 seconds between beats, 240–290
words, overlay text that can't repeat narration, four banned opening patterns.
Constraints are the part you can actually execute.

**Will this make me go viral?**
No. It raises your floor — fewer videos that die at second twenty. Ceiling is
still your story selection, which is what Module 1 is for.

## The Hook Hierarchy (Free)

*Module 2 of Retention Engineering. Free, no email required.*

The first three seconds decide whether the other hundred exist. Here's the
hierarchy I write against instead of guessing.

### Seven tiers, strongest first

1. **Immediate danger** — someone is about to be hurt
2. **Impossible situation** — the setup shouldn't be able to happen
3. **Disturbing mystery** — something is wrong and unexplained
4. **High stakes** — a lot depends on the outcome
5. **Betrayal** — trust breaks
6. **Curiosity gap** — a question with a withheld answer
7. **Emotional conflict** — someone is torn

Aim for tiers 1–3. Most creators default to 6, because curiosity gaps are
the easiest to write — which is exactly why they're the most saturated.

### The four openings to auto-reject

These are what language models produce by default, and what loses viewers:

- **Rhetorical questions.** "What would you do if…" The viewer answers
  internally and leaves.
- **"Imagine…"** Asks for effort before giving a reason to make it.
- **"This is the story of…"** Announces a story instead of starting one.
- **"So…"** Signals a casual anecdote, which sets low stakes.

### The prompt

```
Story premise: [describe it].

Write 8 opening lines, each under 12 words, spoken aloud as the
first thing the viewer hears.

Use this hierarchy, strongest first:
1. Immediate danger  2. Impossible situation  3. Disturbing mystery
4. High stakes  5. Betrayal  6. Curiosity gap  7. Emotional conflict

Label which tier each line uses. At least three must come from
tiers 1-3.

Reject anything that: asks a rhetorical question, starts with
"So", "Imagine", or "This is the story of", or explains context
before the hook lands.
```

### One counterintuitive thing

The highest-tier hook isn't automatically the right one. Running this on a
survival story, the tier-1 option was *"Their plane hit a mountain at twelve
thousand feet."* The chosen line was tier-3: *"They heard the radio say the
search was over."*

The tier-1 line describes an event the viewer pictures instantly — it closes
the loop. The tier-3 line opens one. **Open loops beat strong images.**

# PART C: Reddit Posts

## Post 1 — r/NewTubers

**Title:** Your retention dies in the middle because you never planned where the information lands

**Body:**

Most advice here stops at "strong hook." The hook gets someone to second
three. What loses them is second forty, and that's an architecture problem.

**The rule that changed my retention:** a new reveal, twist, or escalation
every 10–15 seconds. Planned as a beat map *before* writing the script, not
discovered while editing.

Here's the map from a two-minute survival story I made:

- 0:00 Hook
- 0:15 First reveal — something they didn't know at the start
- 0:30 Escalation
- 0:45 Escalation
- 1:00 Reversal
- 1:15 Escalation
- 1:30 Reversal
- 1:45 Resolution

Then the test: **any stretch over 15 seconds with no beat gets flagged and
fixed.** Those flagged timestamps are exactly where your retention graph
shows a cliff.

**The filter that matters more than any of it:** if a beat contains no *new
information*, cut it. Not "no action" — no new information. Restating tension
in different words is the single most common filler in this format, and
viewers detect it instantly even if they couldn't name it.

**Reading your curve** (TikTok gives second-by-second, use it):
- Cliff in the first five seconds → hook problem
- Steady decline through the middle → beat cadence too slow
- Cliff exactly where you placed a reveal → that beat carried nothing new
- Spike → replayed. Either valuable or confusing, go find out which.

Diagnose before rewriting. A hook problem and a pacing problem look identical
in view counts and completely different in the curve.

**Benchmarks worth knowing:** roughly 40–50% average retention is typical
across TikTok. Above 70% completion is where distribution changes character —
that's the number worth chasing, not views.

## Post 2 — r/CapCut *(or your editor's sub)*

**Title:** Writing for TTS is a different skill than writing. Six rules that fixed my audio.

**Body:**

Spent months wondering why my narration sounded off before realising I was
writing for the page and hoping the voice engine would cope.

**The rules I write against now:**

1. **Spell out every number.** "Twenty three," not "23." Engines handle
digits inconsistently and you won't know until you've rendered.
2. **Break any sentence over 15 words.** Long sentences produce unnatural
breath placement.
3. **No em dashes, parentheses, or asterisks.** Either ignored or read aloud.
Both bad.
4. **Commas only where you want an audible pause.** Grammatically correct
commas create pauses that sound wrong. Punctuate for the ear.
5. **No abbreviations.** Write out anything the engine might spell out
letter by letter.
6. **Spoken words only.** No headers, no stage directions, no speaker labels
in the block you paste.

**The check that catches everything else:** read it aloud yourself first.
Anything that makes you stumble makes the engine stumble, and re-rendering
costs more time than proofreading.

**Length target for a two-minute video:** 240–290 words lands at 105–125
seconds at standard pace. Don't speed the TTS up to fit more in — the pauses
are load-bearing, and speeding up flattens the emphasis that makes reveals
land.
