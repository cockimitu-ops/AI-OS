# Competitor Snapshot (Free)

*The first two steps of the Micro-SaaS Moat Blueprint. Free, no email required.*

Most competitor research dies at step one: you list the three competitors
you already knew about and call it a market map. These two modules fix that.

---

## Step 1 — Find everyone

Not the names you know. The ones you don't.

**Paste into any AI assistant with web access** (Perplexity, ChatGPT,
Claude, Gemini — free tiers all work):

```
I'm evaluating a micro-SaaS idea: [describe your idea in 1-2 sentences].

Find every existing product that competes for the same buyer's budget --
direct competitors (same solution), indirect competitors (different
solution, same problem), and adjacent tools users currently duct-tape
together instead. Include small/indie tools, not just market leaders.

For each one give: name, one-line description, pricing model, and how
you found it (search term, directory, forum mention).
```

**Target: 8-15 names.** Fewer than eight usually means the search wasn't
broad enough, not that the niche is empty. Truly empty niches are much rarer
than badly-searched ones.

**Record where each name came from.** If they all came from one listicle,
you have one source's opinion, not a market map.

---

## Step 2 — Tear down the closest four

Pick the four with the closest positioning, most traction, or most similar
pricing. Run this once per competitor:

```
Research [competitor name] in depth. I need:
1. Their exact pricing tiers and what's gated behind each
2. Their core feature list, grouped by category
3. Who their marketing/positioning is clearly built for
4. Any recent product or pricing changes in the last 6 months
5. Public complaints -- search their name plus "alternative", "vs",
   "sucks", or check G2/Reddit/Trustpilot for recurring frustrations

Cite where each finding comes from.
```

**Field 5 is the valuable one.** A complaint that shows up across multiple
independent sources is a gap with proven demand attached. Everything else on
this list is context; the complaints are the opportunity.

---

## One research warning

Ask for citations, then spot-check two of them.

A significant share of "best tools in [category]" articles are published by a
company that ranks itself first. I found this running the process on AI
LinkedIn tools — several of the comparison sites ranking one product #1 were
that product's own blog. The underlying claims held up when checked against
independent sources, but the ranking was worthless.

A competitor map built on one biased listicle is worse than no map, because
you'll trust it.

## What comes next

Those two steps give you a map. They don't tell you what to do with it.

The full Blueprint continues:

**Module 3 — Feature parity matrix.** Every feature as a row, every
competitor as a column. The empty cells are where you're looking.

**Module 4 — Gap sorting.** Three reasons a gap exists: hard to build (real
moat), nobody asked yet (validate first), genuinely low-value (skip). Most
people treat all three as the first one.

**Module 5 — Moat scoring.** Score the wedge 1-5 on data moat, network
effects, switching cost, distribution advantage, and technical complexity.
Under 10: it's a feature, not a company. 10-17: viable, expect a copy in
6-12 months. 18+: compounds.
