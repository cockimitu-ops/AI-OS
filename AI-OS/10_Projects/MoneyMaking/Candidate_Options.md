## Not Financial/Legal/Business Advice
This is a synthesis of research, not a verified plan, and not a substitute for actually testing an idea in reality. Treat it as a strong starting map.

## Reality Check, Kept Honest
Most "solo founder made millions" stories are survivorship bias, or built on an audience that existed for years before the product did. One frequently-cited founder's own numbers: only about 5% of 70+ projects ever made money. German-specific data cuts both ways: only ~33% of solo founders survive 5 years, and the "creative/entertainment" sector specifically has the *lowest* 5-year survival rate of any German industry (~27%) — but the insolvency rate after 3 years is only ~2%, meaning "failure" mostly means giving up, not going bankrupt. For a debt-free digital venture, the real risk is lost time, not financial ruin.

## What's Actually Repeatable
Across multiple real cases, the same few things show up consistently: build in a niche you're already a user of; charge from day one instead of a long free period; build in public; run a portfolio of small bets rather than one big swing; distribution before product; "boring" niches with real willingness-to-pay tend to beat hype niches — one case worth noting is a simple "boring" testimonial-widget product that outperformed its own creator's flashier AI product in stability.

## The Five Ranked Options

### 1. Productized AI content agency for German B2B clients (top recommendation)
Fixed packages for DACH coaches, small companies, or local businesses ("20 AI shorts/month including script, video, editing, analytics report"). Nearly the entire technical side is already covered by existing skills — the real gap is B2B sales (cold outreach, offers, pricing), which can't be shortcut with AI and has to be actively learned. 3–6 months to four-figure monthly revenue if acquisition works. Startable on €50 (just Gewerbeanmeldung, €22–112 in Saxony).

### 2. Self-service AI wrapper around the existing production system
The highest skill-fit software option — the domain knowledge already built (what makes a horror short retention-strong) is the actual moat a generic developer wouldn't have. Economically trickier than #1: variable API costs run ahead of revenue, so it's realistically only comfortable with €500–2,000 in working capital; €50 only supports a prototype. Highly clone-prone. Better framed as a second leg financed by #1's revenue than a starting point.

### 3. Sell automation/system templates (n8n packs, documented content-system templates)
The only option that works fully on €50 with no ongoing fixed costs — selling what's already been documented. **Worth noting directly: this could literally draw on the AI OS itself** — the Context Engine, Execution Engine, and capability framework are exactly the kind of packaged system this option describes. Lowest risk, but more of a supplementary cashflow than a standalone business — comparable products have shown revenue spiking then dropping sharply once a market catches up. Good as a low-cost way to generate first revenue and portfolio proof for #1, not as the end goal.

### 4. Apply for funding, regardless of which option above gets picked
See [[Funding_Opportunities]] — not really a "business option," more the move that removes time pressure from every other option. Flagged as the most important near-term action, ahead of picking a specific idea.

### 5. Content network/media brand — approach with real caution
Fastest start path, no new skill needed, builds directly on the existing niche. But real platform risk: a 2025 policy change threatens channel-wide demonetization for mass-produced, repetitive content — a genuine existential risk for a multi-channel studio model if content gets too template-like. Originality per piece, not "facelessness" itself, is what protects against this. Worth treating as a mid-term second leg alongside #1, not a primary income plan.

## The Honest Overall Read
The strongest asset here isn't the video-making itself — it's having already built a real, documented system, which is exactly what most indie founders reportedly fail to do. The weakest point is the horror content niche specifically: it generates reach, but the audience isn't especially high-paying, and the broader sector has the lowest survival rate in Germany. The leverage is in selling the *production capability* to paying B2B buyers (options 1–3), not in trying to monetize the horror audience directly (option 5). The one skill gap that shows up across every option is sales/acquisition — not anything technical.
