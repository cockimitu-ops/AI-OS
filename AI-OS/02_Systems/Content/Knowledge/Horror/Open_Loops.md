## Core Principles
- An open loop is a specific, structural device: state something surprising, or show a result, without yet explaining it. It's a stronger retention tool than general "mystery," because it's concrete and locatable — the audience knows exactly what they're waiting to find out.
- The honest mechanism is resumption motivation, not enhanced memory. This is often explained through a classic finding that unfinished tasks are remembered better than finished ones — but that specific memory claim has had mixed results under modern scrutiny. What holds up is simpler and still useful: an unresolved question creates a pull to keep going, whether or not it's also remembered better afterward.
- Loops can be stacked. A well-built story rarely runs on one open question — as one closes, a new one opens, so there's rarely a moment with zero pull left. This only works if the story is also actually answering things along the way, not just accumulating open questions.
- A loop has to pay off. The difference between an effective open loop and empty clickbait is delivery — a loop that's opened and never meaningfully closed trains the audience to stop trusting the next one.

## Three Types
1. **Preview loop** — show the result first (the aftermath, the outcome), then go back and explain how it happened.
2. **Gap loop** — state something surprising without yet explaining it.
3. **Suspense loop** — the audience knows something the narrator doesn't yet, and watches them approach finding it out.

## Key Concepts
- Resumption motivation as the honest mechanism (not a memory-enhancement claim)
- Loop stacking (closing one, opening another)
- The trust contract — a loop must eventually deliver
