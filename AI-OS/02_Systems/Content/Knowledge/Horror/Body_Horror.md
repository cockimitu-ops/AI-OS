# Body Horror

Purpose: When and how to use physical wrongness as a horror device, and why it's the least effective tool by default, not the most.

## Core Principles
- There's a rough hierarchy of horror effect, from strongest to weakest: the imagined, unshown threat; the shown-but-not-explicit threat; explicit physical wrongness or gore. Reaching for the third by default, rather than as an escalation after the first two have done their work, undersells the story.
- Body horror works through a specific discomfort: something combining properties that shouldn't coexist — alive and not alive, familiar and foreign in the same object. That combination is what produces the response, not graphic content on its own.
- The uncanny version — almost right, subtly wrong — tends to outperform the overtly grotesque version. A detail that's nearly normal is more disturbing than one that's obviously abnormal.
- The strongest use of body horror in first-person horror specifically is signaling that the threat is no longer external — it's inside the boundary the audience assumed was safe.

## Key Concepts
- The terror/horror/revulsion hierarchy, using the lowest tier (revulsion) sparingly, as an escalation
- Categorical violation (properties that shouldn't coexist) as the actual mechanism
- Uncanny over grotesque
- Internal threat as the strongest application
