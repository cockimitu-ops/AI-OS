# Existential Horror

Purpose: A register of fear distinct from threat-based horror — built on insignificance and indifference rather than danger.
Last Updated: 2026-08-03
Status: Active

## Core Principles
- The strongest version of this register isn't "something wants to hurt me" — it's "something is aware of me and doesn't care." Indifference reads as more unsettling than malice, because malice at least implies the audience matters enough to be a target.
- This register targets a small set of underlying concerns rather than physical danger directly: mortality, meaninglessness, isolation, and loss of control over one's own choices. A story can lean on any one of these without needing a physical threat at all.
- First-person narration is disproportionately effective here specifically, because the collapse between narrator and audience — "I" standing in for the viewer's own perspective — makes insignificance and isolation land as personal rather than observed.

## Key Concepts
- Indifference over malice
- The four underlying concerns: mortality, meaninglessness, isolation, loss of control
- First-person collapse as an amplifier specific to this register
