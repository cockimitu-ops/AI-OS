# Dread and Anticipation

Purpose: Why the period before a scare carries more weight than the scare itself, and how to sustain that period without losing the audience.

Status: Active

## Core Principles
- The imagined threat, sustained, tends to outperform the revealed one. The period of not-yet-knowing is where the strongest response lives; the reveal itself is often a release of tension built earlier, not the source of the tension.
- Suspense is strongest in the interval just before an outcome becomes certain, not at the moment of maximum ambiguity. Tension builds as the bad outcome becomes more likely without yet being confirmed — the closer to certainty without arriving, the stronger the effect.
- Holding a moment of discomfort past the point where it feels comfortable to end it is what makes the eventual release land — cutting away too early leaves the payoff feeling unearned.
- Anticipation of a resolution engages its own reward response, separate from the resolution itself — part of why a well-placed unresolved thread keeps pulling attention even before anything else happens.
- A time constraint — a closing window, something getting audibly closer — converts open-ended uncertainty into urgency, which reads as more active than uncertainty alone.

## Key Concepts
- Anticipation outweighs the event itself
- Suspense peaks just before certainty, not at maximum ambiguity
- Sustaining discomfort longer than feels safe
- Anticipation as its own reward state
- Time pressure as a conversion from uncertainty to urgency
