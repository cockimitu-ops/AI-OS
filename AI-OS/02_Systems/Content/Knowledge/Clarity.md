# Clarity

Purpose: Ensuring content is understood as intended, on first pass, without re-reading or re-hearing.

## Core Principles
- Clarity is measured by the audience's experience, not the writer's intent — if it's misread, it wasn't clear, regardless of what was meant.
- One idea per sentence is easier to follow than several chained together, especially in spoken/TTS formats where re-reading isn't possible.
- Ambiguous pronoun references ("he told him he was wrong") are a common, avoidable clarity failure.

## Key Concepts
- Audience-measured clarity, not writer-intended clarity
- One idea per sentence
- Pronoun ambiguity
